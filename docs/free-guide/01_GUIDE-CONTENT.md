Mariánské Lázně — Marienbad to the German-speaking world that first made it famous — sits at 630 metres above sea level in the wooded hills of West Bohemia. Nearly half the town is forest. ## PAGE 4 — A SHORT HISTORY

**Section label:** THE STORY

**Headline:** Two Hundred Years of Taking the Cure

**Body:**
Marienbad is young by the standards of Bohemian towns. Where Prague counts its history in millennia, Marienbad measures its own from 1808, when the abbot of the nearby Teplá Monastery gave permission to develop the marshy forest clearing where the healing springs rose. Within a generation, drained meadows had become manicured parks, and a spa resort had appeared almost from nothing — planned, deliberate, and elegant.

The town's golden age arrived quickly. By the mid-nineteenth century, Marienbad was one of the most fashionable addresses in Europe, a summer meeting-place for aristocrats, artists, composers and crowned heads. The master gardener Václav Skalník shaped the landscape into the flowing, naturalistic parkland that still defines the town today. Architects raised colonnades, bathhouses and grand hotels in the confident styles of the age — Neo-Renaissance, Art Nouveau, neo-Baroque.

Three guests above all fixed Marienbad in the European imagination. **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** came repeatedly and, at 74, fell famously in love here, pouring the experience into his *Marienbad Elegy*. **Fryderyk Chopin** visited in 1836; the town still honours him each summer with a festival in his name. And **King Edward VII of England** was so devoted a guest that he founded the local golf club — the oldest in the country — in 1905.

**Timeline (vertical, turquoise dots):**
- **1808** — Development of the spa begins under the Teplá Monastery
- **1820s–1840s** — Skalník lays out the parks; the town takes shape
- **1836** — Chopin visits
- **1820s–1823** — Goethe's celebrated summers and the *Marienbad Elegy*
- **1889** — The cast-iron Main Colonnade is completed
- **1905** — King Edward VII founds the Royal Golf Club
- **2021** — Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List

## PAGE 5 — THE HEALING WATERS

**Section label:** THE SPRINGS

**Headline:** The Water That Started It All

**Body:**
Everything in Marienbad begins with the springs. There are around forty within the town and some hundred more in the surrounding countryside — cold, naturally carbonated mineral waters that bubble up at just 7 to 10 °C, each with its own mineral signature and its own traditional use. Sipping them slowly, from a spouted porcelain spa cup, as you stroll the colonnade is the oldest ritual in town, and still the most rewarding.

You don't need a prescription to enjoy the drinking cure, but you should drink with intention: the waters are potent, and each is suited to different needs. Ask at your spa hotel's reception or speak to a spa physician about which springs — and what quantity — are right for you.

**The six springs to know (card grid, turquoise headers):**

- **Cross Spring · Křížový pramen** — The classic Marienbad cure. Traditionally taken for the digestive tract, metabolic disorders, obesity, gout, diabetes and allergies. Housed in the elegant colonnade pavilion at the head of the promenade.
- **Rudolph Spring · Rudolfův pramen** — Prized for the kidneys and urinary tract, for bone health (osteoporosis), and for its calming effect on a stressed mind.
- **Ferdinand Spring · Ferdinandův pramen** — One of the most mineral-rich in the region; used much like the Cross Spring, in its own graceful colonnade a little way from the centre.
- **Forest Spring · Lesní pramen** — Set among the trees, traditionally taken for the upper respiratory tract and the digestive system. A favourite reward at the end of a woodland walk.
- **Ambrose Spring · Ambrožův pramen** — A lighter, iron-bearing water, historically recommended for anaemia and urinary complaints.
- **Caroline Spring · Karolinin pramen** — Associated with kidney stones and, in particular, oxalate stones.

**Callout box (beige):**
**The spa cup.** The porcelain cup with the flat handle-spout isn't a souvenir gimmick — it's designed so you sip the mineral water slowly and at the right pace. Buy one when you arrive; it's the most Marienbad thing you can own.

## PAGE 6 — MARIENBAD'S NATURAL TREASURES

**Section label:** WHAT MAKES IT WORK

**Headline:** Three Gifts from the Earth

**Body:**
The springs are the most visible of Marienbad's natural healing resources, but they are not the only ones. The same volcanic geology that carbonates the water produces two more treasures found in few other places on earth — and, above it all, a mountain climate that is itself considered a form of treatment.

**Maria's Gas — the town's signature (feature box, indigo background, white text):**
Deep beneath Marienbad, volcanic activity releases a natural gas that is an astonishing **99.7 % pure carbon dioxide**. Drawn from Maria's Spring, *Maria's Gas* is used in dry gas baths and in subcutaneous gas injections — treatments that support circulation and are prized in cardiovascular and rehabilitation care. This resource is **exclusive to Ensana's Marienbad spa** and available nowhere else in the world in this form.

**Peloids — the healing peat:**
The wetlands around Marienbad yield peat and peat mud (*rašelina* and *slatina*), applied warm at 38–42 °C as wraps and baths. Rich in organic acids and minerals, peloid therapy soothes joints and muscles and is a mainstay of treatment for the musculoskeletal system.

**The climate itself:**
At 630 metres, ringed by the forests of the Slavkov Wood, Marienbad enjoys a *submontane toning climate* — clean, cool, gently stimulating air that has been formally recognised as a climatic-spa resource since 2023. Simply breathing here, day after day, is part of the cure.

## PAGE 12 — THINGS TO DO

**Section label:** ABOVE THE WATER

**Headline:** More Than a Spa Town

**Body:**
Marienbad has always offered more than the cure. A single afternoon or a full week — either way you'll not run short of things to do, and nearly all of it unfolds against a backdrop of extraordinary natural beauty.

**The must-sees (numbered):**
1. **The Singing Fountain** — In front of the Main Colonnade, a choreographed display of water, light and music plays every odd hour, most magically after dark. The repertoire runs from Dvořák and Chopin to popular melodies. Arrive a few minutes early for a good spot.
2. **The Main Colonnade** — The cast-iron masterpiece of 1889, sheltering springs and strollers alike. The ironwork and painted ceilings reward a slow look; it is beautiful in every season.
3. **The Church of the Assumption** — Its blue-and-white façade crowns a hill above the centre, with fine views over the rooftops and forests.
4. **The spa parks and forest trails** — Laid out by Václav Skalník, flowing straight from the colonnade into dozens of kilometres of marked woodland paths.
5. **The Geological Park** — A short walk from the centre, telling the story of the volcanic forces that made the springs.
6. **The Hamelika lookout tower** — A rewarding uphill walk to a panorama over the whole spa valley.

## PAGE 13 — ACTIVE MARIENBAD

**Section label:** GET MOVING

**Headline:** Golf, Trails and Fresh Air

**Body:**
The clean submontane air that heals is also an invitation to move. Marienbad has been an active resort since Edwardian times, and today it's one of the finest bases in Bohemia for golf, cycling and walking.

**Royal Golf Club Mariánské Lázně (feature box):**
Founded in **1905 by King Edward VII of England**, this is the **oldest golf course in the Czech Republic** — an 18-hole championship layout winding through pine forest high above the town. Visitors are welcome; the Edwardian clubhouse is a destination in itself, and equipment hire is on site.

**Also on offer (icon list):**
- **Tennis & padel** — courts in the spa district and at several hotels.
- **Cycling & mountain biking** — trails for every level through the Slavkov Forest; bikes for hire in town.
- **Hiking & Nordic walking** — a vast network of marked paths straight from the parks.
- **Disc golf** — an 18-hole public course.
- **Winter sports** — cross-country and downhill skiing at the Mariánky ski area when snow allows; skating on the frozen spa ponds.

## PAGE 15 — DAY TRIPS

**Section label:** BEYOND THE TOWN

**Headline:** Five Trips Worth the Drive

**Body:**
Marienbad's position in the Slavkov Forest, a short hop from the German border, makes it a natural base for exploring West Bohemia and beyond.

**The trips (card grid with distance tags):**
- **Karlovy Vary (Carlsbad)** — *~50 km* — Bohemia's other great spa town, with its own colonnades, hot springs and the famous Becherovka.
- **Teplá Monastery** — The Premonstratensian abbey that once owned the Marienbad springs, with an outstanding historic library.
- **Loket Castle** — A dramatic medieval fortress above the Ohře river; summer festivals and superb views.
- **Kladská Nature Reserve** — *~15 min* — A pristine peat bog with boardwalk trails and a picture-postcard hunting lodge.
- **Plzeň (Pilsen)** — The birthplace of Pilsner beer, with a brewery tour and one of Europe's largest Renaissance squares.

**Callout (turquoise):**
**Crossing into Bavaria.** The border is close enough for a spontaneous afternoon — historic Waldsassen and its basilica, or the volcanic hill of Parkstein, are both easy runs from town.

## PAGE 16 — SEASON BY SEASON

**Section label:** WHEN TO COME

**Headline:** There Is No Wrong Season

**Body:**
Marienbad rewards a visit at any time of year — but each season has its own character. Here's how to choose.

**Four panels (one per season, seasonal colour tint):**

**Spring** — The town wakes. The parks green, the fountain sings again, and the ceremonial opening of the spa season fills the streets. Fewer crowds, soft light, everything freshly painted. *Best for: renewal, quiet cures, photography.*

**Summer** — The high season. Warm days, full terraces, festivals in the colonnade, golf in the long evenings and every spring flowing. *Best for: culture, activity, first-time visitors.*

**Autumn** — Arguably the loveliest. The forests turn gold and copper, the air sharpens, and the spa settles into a contemplative calm. *Best for: walking, romance, a serious cure.*

**Winter** — The secret season. Snow softens the colonnades, the pools steam invitingly, and treatment rooms are at their cosiest. *Best for: value, warmth-in-the-cold, digital detox.*

## PAGE 18 — PRACTICAL INFORMATION

**Section label:** GOOD TO KNOW

**Headline:** Before You Come

**[EDITORIAL NOTE]** Two-column practical panel, turquoise sub-headers.

**Getting here**
- **By air:** Nearest major airports are Nuremberg (~170 km), Prague (~160 km) and Munich (~280 km).
- **By car:** Berlin and Vienna are each about 420 km; Frankfurt ~410 km; Karlovy Vary just 50 km away.
- **By train:** Marienbad sits on the Prague–Cheb line, with its handsome historic station a short ride from the spa district.

**When to visit**
- Peak season is May–September; the fountain and full festival programme run through the warmer months. Autumn is quieter and beautiful; winter offers the best value.

**Practical notes**
- **Currency:** Czech koruna (CZK); cards accepted widely.
- **Language:** Czech; German and English widely spoken in the spa hotels.
- **The drinking cure:** Free to enjoy at the colonnade — buy a spa cup on arrival and sip slowly.
- **Altitude & climate:** 630 m; cool, fresh air. Bring layers and comfortable walking shoes even in summer.
- **Getting around:** The spa district is compact and walkable; most hotels are steps from the colonnade.
